# Art direction before page construction

Use this for new designs, material visual redesigns, and concept adaptation. Scale it down for fixed design systems or narrowly scoped tasks. Build a concrete visual brief before writing a section-by-section implementation plan.

## Establish what matters

Inspect the project and read existing compiled reference descriptions before asking questions. Recover prior preferences and approvals. If art direction is open, apply Recommended directions: offer to source project-fit examples and proactively present three linked directions when references are absent, rather than asking the user to provide them. Separate actual restrictions from inherited defaults and the agent's assumptions. An existing dashboard theme does not automatically govern a public website. Permission to diverge allows exploration within that surface without changing shared tokens elsewhere.

Resolve these decisions from evidence or concise, consequential questions.

| Decision | Useful evidence or question |
|---|---|
| Audience and message | Who must understand or accomplish what? What needs to be clear in the opening view? Which product fact differentiates this offer? |
| Desired experience | Which supplied reference best captures the intended feeling, and which specific part matters? Identify what the user dislikes about the current design. |
| Design freedom | What is fixed, what may change, and what must remain compatible? Separate logo, public brand, embedded product UI, and application theme. |
| Content and assets | What authentic product views, photography, illustration, brand assets, and copy exist? What is missing for the proposed composition? |
| Motion and constraints | Is motion part of the brief or only feedback? What devices, reduced-motion behavior, technical limits, access, and budget constrain it? |

Do not ask the user to pick fonts, arbitrary hex values, animation milliseconds, or a library without first offering a reasoned visual proposal. Ask about audience, positioning, preferences, or constraints only when project evidence cannot resolve them. Question count is not a measure of discovery quality.

## Analyze visual references

For already compiled images, use the stored description. Inspect newly supplied, unprocessed images once when their extraction is part of the task. For motion references, inspect temporal states or a recording when tools support it. If unavailable, name the missing evidence rather than inventing motion. Use a small set of relevant references, preferring the user's examples over unrelated trends.

Record a compact reference map with source, concrete visual observation, transferable principle, and intended application. Examples of observations are a large type-to-detail contrast, asymmetrical hero balance, tightly grouped product evidence, restrained neutral surfaces with a concentrated accent, or a staged product reveal. “Modern,” “premium,” and “clean” are goals, not observations.

Carry over principles with an original composition. Do not copy brands, fabricated business proof, proprietary illustration, or a screenshot's tiny text and desktop proportions into production. Distinguish the presentation background around a mockup from the application's actual UI.

Before code, complete the reference-calibration table. Preserve the intended aesthetic across alternatives; different product metaphors do not justify unrelated visual languages. An assumed warm/retro aesthetic is not a neutral fallback for contemporary references.

## Define a visual hypothesis for each direction

Before expanding a full page, make a representative high-fidelity composition using truthful content. For marketing, include the hero and one substantive proof or product section. For product UI, include the main task with realistic populated data and one important alternate state. Include the compact layout. A text-only mood description is insufficient to settle visual identity.

For each candidate specify the following in a short design brief.

- An organizing idea tied to the user's task or product truth. Explain what makes the direction identifiable beyond its logo.
- A layout sketch with focal point, reading order, dominant scale relationships, content width, gutters, text measure, and negative space.
- A proposed palette with actual semantic values and roles. State where the accent is concentrated and how status colors remain distinct. Validate adjacent contrast pairs during review.
- Typography with actual family or available fallback, weights, sizes, line heights, and intentional headline wrapping at the target widths. Choose type for the composition and content, not because a starter uses it.
- Spacing rhythm, control sizes, radii by role, borders, shadow or depth treatment, icon family, and alignment anchors. Explain deviations from the inherited system.
- A visual asset strategy. Choose real product evidence, existing licensed imagery, proposed generated imagery, or code-native graphics for their role. Missing assets remain visible design dependencies.
- A motion storyboard when requested or central to the concept, with entry, change, settled state, trigger, timing, mobile handling, interruption, and static/reduced-motion equivalent. A hover lift or short tab fade alone does not fulfill a request for a motion-led landing page.

Do not impose “no new dependencies,” “no images,” “everything static,” a hard asset limit, or an assumed LCP element as convenience rules. Derive budgets and stack decisions from actual project constraints and verify performance claims after implementation. Propose imagery and request the required permission if it would materially improve the result. A CSS blob or invented product panel is not an automatic replacement for a missing visual asset.

## Make directions meaningfully different

When visual identity is open, vary at least two substantive dimensions such as composition, typography, color distribution, image treatment, depth/shape, density, or motion language. Keep audience, product facts, task, and evaluation conditions comparable. Do not freeze the incumbent's entire visual language and call two section arrangements a brand exploration.

When the brand is fixed, explore within its real boundaries. State which variables are legitimately open. If the user already selected a concept, refine that concept instead of manufacturing alternatives.

Build the strongest version of each candidate at equal fidelity. The selection deliverable is a complete landing page or representative end-to-end app task for every candidate; the earlier calibrated slice is only an internal milestone. Do not make a deliberately weak option to validate a preferred one. Inspect and improve representative views before multiplying pages and sections. Expand enough content to judge the whole experience where the user's decision depends on page rhythm or navigation.

For marketing, assign each section a distinct communication job and composition suited to its content. Review the full-page silhouette for repetitive framed panels, uniform bands, excessive empty gaps, and monotonous scale. Product-forward means product evidence supports understanding. It does not require embedding a complete dashboard repeatedly.

For product UI, prioritize predictable task structure, useful density, readable data, and state clarity. Distinctiveness can come from excellent information design and crafted details. Do not force marketing theatrics onto frequent tasks.

## Resolve conflicts as the design owner

Treat generic aesthetic heuristics as guidance. A universal color ban cannot overrule an approved brand or contextual evidence. Explain and record the applicable rule once. Do not ask the user to adjudicate whether indigo counts as violet or to resolve disagreements between skill authors.

Use component catalogs to solve concrete needs or evaluate a useful signature element. Do not force adoption to prove research happened. Normalize adopted items to the chosen design language and review their rendered state. Catalog polish does not establish whole-page quality.

Recommend a candidate only after visual review. If a hybrid would materially change either candidate, render and review the combined composition before recommending it as the finished direction. Do not hide a third, untested design behind the word “hybrid.”
